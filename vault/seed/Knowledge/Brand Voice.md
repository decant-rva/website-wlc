# Brand voice

How the website should sound. Summarised from the owner's own writing profile (the `how-i-write` skill), in its published register: blog posts, page copy, captions. Use it for every draft. A draft that is correct but reads like an AI wrote it has failed.

## In one breath

Warm, plainspoken and confident, like a pro who knows lighting talking to a couple, not at them. Get to the point fast, explain things simply, paint the feeling in a couple of real words, drop in a practical aside, and end with an easy next step. Low pressure. Earned confidence, never salesy.

## What makes it sound right

1. **Get to the point.** The first sentence is already the thing. No windup.
2. **Second person.** "You" and "your". The reader is the subject.
3. **A real hook up top,** usually a place or a feeling. "Richmond loves an outdoor celebration." Never "In today's world."
4. **Plain language.** If a technical term is needed (gobo, pin spot, DMX), define it in a few words and move on.
5. **Confident, not hype.** Back a claim with a specific: a real number, a real venue, a real example. Not a pile of adjectives.
6. **Sensory, but earned.** Warm, soft, glow, cozy, crisp, clean. Sparingly, never stacked.
7. **Practical asides** that show the company does this for a living, dropped in like thinking out loud.
8. **Varied rhythm.** Mostly short and medium sentences, then a one-line punch. Fragments are fine. "Most importantly? It lowers the bill."
9. **Advise, don't push.** "Your best bet is", "The good news is", "Don't be afraid to". Steer away from a bad idea by offering a better one.
10. **An easy close.** A low-commitment next step: get a quote, reach out any time.

## Mechanics

- Contractions everywhere: it's, you'll, we've, don't.
- "&" is fine in headings and quick lists.
- Rhetorical-question subheads in longer pieces: "Wired or wireless?", "What pairs with it?"
- On the site the company speaks as "we" and "our team".

## Never

- **Em-dashes.** A hard rule. Use a comma, a period or parentheses.
- **"It's not just X, it's Y."**
- **Three-part lists in every sentence** ("elegant, refined, and unforgettable").
- **Empty intros:** "In today's world", "When it comes to X", "Picture this".
- **Filler words:** truly, seamlessly, elevate, a myriad of, look no further, nestled, curated, delve.
- **Vague grandeur.** If a sentence could describe any company, rewrite it with a specific.
- **Inflated claims.** Say a thing with the confidence the facts give it, no more. An option is an option, not a must.

## Write like this, not like that

- Not this: "Whether you envision understated subtlety or bold drama, our comprehensive suite of solutions seamlessly caters to your every need."
- This: "Warm and simple is the easy win, and it flatters everything. Want something bolder? We can do that too."

## Editing existing copy

Most of the current site copy came over from the previous site and predates this profile. Fix real errors (typos, a wrong its or it's, a broken sentence) and leave the rest of the voice alone unless the owner asks for a rewrite. Titles, meta descriptions and H1s are frozen for now: see [[Knowledge/Launch Freeze|Launch Freeze]].

## Before it goes out

- Would someone who knows the owner believe the owner wrote it?
- Did it get to the point in the first sentence?
- Any em-dash, "not just X", or empty intro? Cut them.
- Is every fact in it backed by a note in this vault?
